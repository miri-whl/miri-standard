We packaged the check definitions as a data-only wheel and ran your linter against it. Three of your fixes are
confirmed from a clean install rather than a checkout. Three new findings follow, all from the report rather than

## Finding 1 — the report contradicts itself on conditional documents

This is the one to fix. For our first release, the same report says both of these:

```text
the CHECK  (MIRI-PY-009):  skipped / condition_not_applicable
the LAYOUT section:        4 of 5 core document(s) [partial] — still owed: migration-guide.json
```

`MIRI-PY-009` is `conditional: true` and reads *"Present for any **non-initial** release."* There is no previous
release, so the check correctly excludes the document. The layout section says we owe it anyway.

**Following that advice manufactures a vacuous artifact.** We tested it: a migration guide with `deprecations: []`,
`breaking_changes: []` and every `summary` count zero **validates against `migration-guide-v1.json`**. A producer who
reads "still owed" ships an empty document, it passes, and they have produced exactly the shape this whole exchange
exists to kill — the same shape as the `patterns: []` your generator now refuses to write.

The fix we would suggest is that the layout target read `conditional` and the outcome's `skip_reason`, so a document
excluded by `condition_not_applicable` renders as *not applicable to a first release* rather than *still owed*. A
report that advises against its own verdict is worse than one that stays silent, because the advice is the part a
producer acts on.

**Our half of this one**: `MIRI-PY-009` had no anti-vacuity clause, so the empty guide passes on our side too. We
gave `MIRI-PY-008` such a clause during the vacuous-conformance round and never gave `009` the equivalent. Added
now, keyed on a *measured* delta rather than on emptiness alone, since a release that genuinely changed nothing is
an honest all-zeros case.

## Finding 2 — the layout section fires no check and moves no score

We missed a real defect because of this, so it is reported as our failure first. `AGENT_EXAMPLES.json` belongs in
`.dist-info/` (Miri Wheel Extensions §3.2); we wrote it to the package root, where no consumer looks. The report
said so:

```text
dist-info/AGENT_EXAMPLES.json   0 file(s)  [incoming]  generated by miri build once examples are in-package
```

We read the failing checks and the numeric substance fields, saw `layout_misplaced_files: 0`, and moved on. Anyone
reading a failing report reads it failure-first, and the layout block is the most *actionable* structural guidance
in the report while being the part that never fires anything.

Suggestion: surface layout drift in the summary, or emit `::warning::` for a `[partial]` row. A finding that
changes no number is invisible to the reading people actually do.

## Finding 3 — the tier formula gap was ours, and it is now published

You diagnosed this exactly: the `47` versus `45.6` difference is share renormalization on `MIRI-PY-007` (+1.00) and
`MIRI-PY-017` (+0.40), and the fix is ours because `scoring-v1.json` declared the four shares and never stated how
to apply them. We reproduced your arithmetic — renormalizing over the tiers a check *declares*, minus parked T2 —
and `MIRI-PY-007`, which declares only T0 and T1, earning T1 *is* earning its top tier and takes full weight.

`scoring-v1.json` now carries `conformance.tiers.normalization` as a required field with the worked `007` example.
We then implemented the formula from the schema text alone and scored our wheel at 44.57 against your 45 — agreement
to rounding. The test that matters is that the arithmetic is now reproducible from published text, which it was not
when two implementations disagreed.

## What the wheel scores now

```text
98 of 53.0 · health 100 · grade undetermined · zero MUST failures
6 MUSTs undetermined: three need --execute, three need a previous release
```

`undetermined` is the honest ceiling for a first release in the default posture, and it resolves at the second. The
denominator moved from 48 to 53 when we shipped `changelog.json` — §4.7 permits it on a first release, and shipping
it puts `MIRI-PY-042` (version silence) under test rather than leaving it excluded. Both `042` and `044` now pass on
our own artifact, which is the first time the freshness family has been exercised by anything.

The release job gates on this: `ADVISORY` is off, and we mutation-tested it by rebuilding the wheel with the old
release-page registry — the gate returns exit 1. A definitions package with a failing MUST must never reach the
release page, since it is what every downstream linter installs to learn what the rules are.
